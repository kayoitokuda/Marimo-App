Expand the existing Marimo accountability app by weaving a cohesive narrative world throughout the current product.

This is an update to the existing app, not a rebuild. Preserve all existing screens, routes, working interactions, responsive behavior, local data, commitment logic, Future Fund mechanics, history, simulation controls, and uploaded Marimo mascot assets.

Do not replace the existing visual system or remove functionality. Use the narrative direction below to refine the product’s screen flows, copy, states, transitions, and Marimo interactions.

CORE PRODUCT PREMISE

Marimo is an accountability platform disguised as a funny cat app.

The user makes a real promise to their future self and attaches a bounty to it. Marimo, a small but disturbingly serious cat executive, supervises the agreement.

If the user completes the commitment:

- The promise is officially honored
- The bounty is protected
- The bounty contributes to the user’s selected Future Fund
- The user builds their reliability streak
- Marimo receives a treat and acknowledges the user’s competence

If the user misses the commitment:

- The agreement is marked breached
- Marimo claims the bounty
- The bounty is ceremonially yeeted into the stratosphere
- The funds are humorously described as being allocated to Marimo’s retirement, treats, executive compensation, or other suspicious corporate expenses
- The user is offered a practical recovery path

The deeper emotional story is:

“You are not being punished for failing at productivity. You are practicing keeping promises to your future self.”

The product should help users move from vague intentions to specific, achievable commitments with meaningful stakes.

THE WORLD OF MARIMO

The product itself is called “Marimo.”

“Marimo Inc.” is the fictional institution operating inside the app.

Marimo Inc. is an absurdly formal accountability corporation run by one cat. It has:

- One CEO
- One employee
- No visible board of directors
- A suspicious treat budget
- An orbital bounty disposal program
- Extremely serious commitment paperwork
- A vague but rapidly growing retirement fund
- Strong opinions about incomplete portfolio case studies

Marimo is simultaneously:

- Chief Executive Officer
- Chief Accountability Officer
- Compliance department
- Evidence reviewer
- Treat recipient
- Bounty disposal specialist

The user is not Marimo’s pet and should not be treated like a child. The user is a capable adult entering into a formal agreement with their future self. Marimo acts as the neutral but mildly judgmental administrator of that agreement.

The joke is that an ordinary personal commitment is treated with the procedural seriousness of a corporate contract, while the corporation itself is clearly being operated by a fluffy cat.

NARRATIVE TONE

The writing should be:

- Deadpan
- Concise
- Warm underneath the sarcasm
- Slightly passive-aggressive
- Bureaucratically absurd
- Never cruel or genuinely shaming
- Funny because it is delivered seriously

Marimo should never call the user lazy, irresponsible, pathetic, or bad.

The humor should target:

- Procrastination logic
- Corporate bureaucracy
- Startup economics
- Performance reviews
- Executive compensation
- Suspicious financial reallocations
- Marimo’s confidence in her own authority

Avoid:

- Baby talk
- Excessive cat puns
- Meme language everywhere
- Making Marimo constantly talk
- Long paragraphs of joke copy
- Treating the user as an employee being abused by a boss
- Making failure feel humiliating
- Turning the interface into a pet-care game
- Making Marimo overly expressive or cheerful

Marimo is funniest when she remains calm and completely convinced that she is in charge.

NARRATIVE PRINCIPLE

The interface should operate on two simultaneous layers:

FUNCTIONAL LAYER

Use plain, trustworthy product language for anything the user must understand or act on:

- Create commitment
- Definition of done
- Deadline
- Bounty at stake
- Future Fund
- Submit completion
- Commitment completed
- Deadline missed
- Try again
- Break this into a smaller commitment

NARRATIVE LAYER

Use Marimo Inc. language in supporting copy, status messages, transitions, empty states, receipts, and outcome moments:

- Management has been informed.
- Reviewing contractual obligations…
- Evaluating potential excuses…
- Compliance is monitoring the situation.
- Evidence has been forwarded for review.
- Marimo acknowledges your competence.
- Funds have been allocated accordingly.
- The bounty has left the atmosphere.

Never allow the joke layer to make an important action or financial outcome unclear.

THE CORE NARRATIVE ARC

Build every commitment around five acts:

1. INTENTION

The user identifies something their future self genuinely needs.

This should feel reflective and optimistic, not threatening.

Prompt examples:

- What promise would make Future You’s life easier?
- What have you been meaning to finish?
- Make the commitment specific enough to keep.

2. CONTRACT

The vague intention becomes a concrete agreement:

- Task
- Definition of done
- Deadline
- Evidence
- Bounty
- Successful destination
- Missed consequence

This is when Marimo becomes involved.

3. SUPERVISION

The commitment is active.

Marimo observes without constantly interrupting. The interface focuses on the task, time remaining, definition of done, and bounty.

4. VERDICT

The user either submits completion or reaches the deadline.

This moment should feel ceremonial and satisfying.

5. OUTCOME AND RECOVERY

Success contributes to Future You.

Failure becomes information. Marimo performs the bounty yeet, but the product then helps the user adjust and recommit.

ONBOARDING NARRATIVE

Refine onboarding so it introduces this world quickly.

SCREEN 1 — THE PROMISE

Headline:

“Keep a promise to Future You.”

Supporting copy:

“Choose something important, set a deadline, and place a bounty on the outcome.”

Marimo may be present quietly, observing.

SCREEN 2 — THE AGREEMENT

Explain the loop clearly:

“Finish in time, and your bounty goes toward something you want. Miss the deadline, and Marimo reallocates it.”

Include a small visual showing:

Commitment → Completion → Future Fund

and:

Commitment → Missed deadline → Orbital reallocation

Optional Marimo note:

Example funds:

- New Laptop

- Art Studio
- Extremely Responsible Purchase
- Something Marimo Would Not Approve

The fund is not a general wallet. It represents the future reward connected to promises kept.

SCREEN 5 — FIRST COMMITMENT

Move immediately into creating the user’s first real commitment.

No separate tutorial completion screen is necessary.

CREATE COMMITMENT FLOW

Make the commitment flow feel like an agreement being drafted with Marimo Inc.

STEP 1 — DECLARE THE PROMISE

Title:

“What are you committing to?”

Supporting prompt:

“Choose one outcome you can clearly finish.”

When the user enters something specific, Marimo may respond:

- “Specific. Acceptable.”
- “Management understands the deliverable.”
- “This appears measurable.”
- “A real task. Promising.”

If it is too vague, offer helpful clarification:

- “What would ‘done’ look like?”
- “Marimo cannot enforce ‘work on portfolio.’”
- “Try naming the exact section you will finish.”

STEP 2 — DEFINE DONE

Title:

“What counts as complete?”

Supporting copy:

“Decide now, before Deadline You begins negotiating.”

Evidence options:

- I’ll confirm it myself
- Submit a link
- Upload a screenshot
- Reach a measurable target

Marimo note:

“Terms may not be reinterpreted retroactively.”

STEP 3 — SET THE DEADLINE

Title:

“When is this due?”

Show the exact date, time, and resulting duration.

Contextual comments may include:

- “A reasonable allocation of time.”
- “Ambitious, but technically possible.”
- “Nine hours. Management will permit it.”
- “Three weeks is beginning to resemble a wish.”

Do not block the user based on Marimo’s opinion.

STEP 4 — SET THE BOUNTY

Title:

“What is this promise worth?”

Clearly show both outcomes:

IF COMPLETED

“$25 goes toward Japan Trip.”

IF MISSED

“Marimo claims $25.”

Supporting copy:

“The bounty should be noticeable enough to matter, but not harmful to lose.”

Marimo’s attention may subtly increase as the amount increases.

Possible comments:

- $5: “A modest contractual interest.”
- $10: “Management is now attentive.”
- $25: “Marimo has joined the meeting.”
- Higher amount: “Please confirm this remains financially comfortable.”

Do not pressure the user into increasing the amount.

STEP 5 — REVIEW THE AGREEMENT

Present a formal but readable summary.

Possible label:

“Commitment Agreement No. 0001”

Include:

- Promise
- Definition of done
- Deadline
- Bounty
- Future Fund
- Missed consequence
- Evidence method
- Reminder schedule

Primary action:

“Accept commitment”

Secondary action:

“Revise terms”

Small print:

“By accepting, you confirm that the task is achievable, the deadline is intentional, and Marimo has been granted entirely too much authority.”

COMMITMENT ACCEPTANCE SEQUENCE

After confirmation, play a brief narrative sequence:

- Reviewing terms…
- Searching for obvious loopholes…
- Evaluating excuse exposure…
- Informing management…
- Commitment accepted.

Show Marimo applying a paw-print or formal approval stamp.

Final message:

“Commitment accepted. Marimo has been informed.”

ACTIVE COMMITMENT WORLD

The active commitment screen should feel calm and focused, not like the user is trapped inside a corporate joke.

Primary hierarchy:

1. Task
2. Deadline and time remaining
3. Bounty
4. Definition of done
5. Submit completion action
6. Future Fund destination
7. Marimo’s supervision status

Possible supervision statuses:

- Management is monitoring the situation.
- Marimo is presently withholding judgment.
- No compliance concerns at this time.
- Plenty of time remains. Allegedly.
- Deadline proximity has been noted.
- Marimo has entered the attentive loaf position.
- Executive oversight has increased.

Marimo should mostly sit, loaf, observe, or obstruct something harmless. Do not animate her constantly.

MIDWAY

NEAR DEADLINE

FINAL WINDOW

Never hide the actual deadline beneath joke copy.

COMPLETION AND EVIDENCE REVIEW

When the user finishes:

Primary action:

“Submit completion”

Evidence submission copy:

“Send the agreed evidence for review.”

After submission, show a short Marimo Inc. review sequence:

- Evidence received.
- Verifying contractual terms…
- Inspecting suspicious pixels…
- Consulting management…
- Determining treat eligibility…

Marimo may inspect the evidence from an unreasonably close distance.

The review is theatrical in the prototype. Make it clear when evidence is self-attested or automatically accepted.

RECOVERY AFTER FAILURE

Immediately follow the theatrical consequence with a useful recovery experience.

Headline:

“What made this commitment difficult?”

Options:

- The task was too large
- The deadline was unrealistic
- I forgot
- I lost motivation
- Something unexpected happened
- I was unclear about what counted as done
- Other

Then recommend a next step.

Primary recovery action:

“Make it smaller and recommit”

Other actions:

- Try the same commitment again
- Choose a more realistic deadline
- Lower the bounty
- Schedule the first step
- Return home

Example transformation:

ORIGINAL

“Finish portfolio case study”

RECOMMENDED

“Rewrite the opening problem section”

Marimo commentary should become less sarcastic here:

- “Revise the terms. Continue.”
- “A smaller promise is still a real promise.”
- “Management supports improved forecasting.”
- “The objective remains available.”

The recovery flow is essential. The product should teach better commitment design, not merely collect failures.

MARIMO’S CHARACTER BEHAVIOR

Marimo’s personality should be conveyed through restraint.

DEFAULT BEHAVIOR

- Sitting formally
- Loafing near the commitment
- Looking at the deadline
- Watching the bounty
- Blocking a harmless decorative element
- Sitting too close to evidence
- Slowly turning toward suspicious activity

SUCCESS BEHAVIOR

- Receives treat
- Performs one absurd spin
- Applies approval stamp
- Pretends the success was expected
- Briefly reveals excitement, then returns to composure

FAILURE BEHAVIOR

- Becomes very still
- Enters with procedural seriousness
- Collects bounty
- Performs dramatic yeet
- Watches it disappear
- Returns to business

LARGE AMOUNTS

- Becomes increasingly attentive
- Tail becomes visible
- Sits closer
- Treat icon appears
- Does not encourage unsafe spending

CANCEL ATTEMPT

- Slowly places paw near the cancellation area
- Requests a reason for the quarterly report
- Ultimately allows the user to cancel

Marimo should never prevent access, obscure important information, or make the app frustrating to use.

VISUAL STORYTELLING

Keep the existing clean, accountability-forward interface.

The ordinary product should feel:

- Calm
- Credible
- Spacious
- Focused
- Financially legible

The fictional corporate world should appear through:

- Official-looking labels
- Stamps
- Receipts
- Status messages
- Small annotations
- Marimo’s posture
- Carefully timed animation
- Brief cinematic transitions

Do not cover every screen in corporate motifs. The narrative should feel like a layer the user gradually discovers.

The serious product and the absurd world must strengthen each other:

- The serious hierarchy makes the joke funnier
- The humor makes the accountability less intimidating
- The Future Fund gives the system emotional meaning
- The bounty makes the promise tangible
- The recovery flow makes the product genuinely useful

FINAL PRODUCT FEELING

The app should feel like a legitimate accountability system accidentally governed by a fluffy cat with startup-founder confidence.

The user should come away thinking:

“This is ridiculous, but it may actually make me finish the thing.”

Apply this narrative consistently across the existing onboarding, commitment creation, active commitment, completion, missed deadline, recovery, Future Fund, history, profile, and share states without breaking current functionality.
